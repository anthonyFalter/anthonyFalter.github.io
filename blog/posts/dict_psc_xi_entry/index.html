How we fared against DICT's 11th Startup Competition

November 12, 2026, 2:48 PM · Ross Elopre



Recently, we decided to give DICT's 11th Startup Competition a try to experience what founders go through when pitching their ideas. As our very first startup competition, we were nervous during the initial events and everything that followed. Even so, we were able to learn a lot and gain valuable experience along the way.

One of our key achievements during this competition was securing a placement in the top 20 of the regional lineups—which, for us, was a significant accomplishment as first-timers in this arena.



Coming up with an innovation was already difficult, but successfully defining our value proposition proved to be an even bigger challenge. It forced us to search for creative ways to deliver our solution to our target market.

During our ideation stages, we proposed several ideas targeting sectors like agriculture, education, and legal-tech. We ultimately steered away from agriculture because it is highly saturated; as a team of newbies, we needed to think outside the box to carve out a unique value proposition. What we ended up building was a Legal-Tech Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) solution tailored for both civilians and lawyers.



We started by researching the market and identifying the specific pain points both groups experience: the lengthy time it takes to address legal issues for practitioners, and the lack of accessible legal resources for civilians.

We wanted to offer a solution that makes legal workflows more efficient by introducing a triage system for case intakes, alongside an intelligent way to prioritize cases based on a lawyer's specialty and urgent markers like immediate litigation or emergencies.

While developing our concept, we hit a lot of hurdles—the biggest being that every feature we implemented had to strictly adhere to the Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) regulations and the Data Privacy Act of 2012.



As team leader, I had to navigate the technical hurdles my members faced while keeping our solution within strict legal boundaries, all while ensuring that each of us grew from the experience.

One of the most valuable lessons I learned is that leadership isn't just about giving directions—it's about supporting your team through every roadblock so you can all grow together.

We decided to go the extra mile and develop an early-stage MVP for our legal-tech solution, which I genuinely believe is what pushed us into the Top 20 startups.



When the regional competitions rolled around, we were excited to showcase our solution to the judges. Things went surprisingly smoothly, and the other startups were genuinely impressive. Our team was thrilled to be part of the event, and we walked away having learned a ton from everyone on how to pitch our ideas in the best way possible.

While we may have not won during the regionals, we were proud of our performance and the progress we made. On to the next chapter, BatasGO!
